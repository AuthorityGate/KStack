## Ruling

**Option 1 selected**: fold reclamation into round 31's single, never-split append-lock hold as its **first step**. Reclaim obligations are recomputed from `(durable chain prefix + directory listing)` at every hold and are **never persisted** as a separate record. All auxiliary records introduced across rounds 29-31 are retired: `StagingIntent`, `CleanupTombstone`, `SlotAttempt`, the pre-allocated `refId`, and the `CROSS-RENAME-INSTALL-DURABLE` primitive.

**Option 2 (a monotonic epoch/generation counter) was rejected**: an epoch-based design still needs delete/adopt mutual exclusion between a reader and a deleter, so it degenerates to "the lock plus a redundant record" — no simpler than Option 1, with extra state to keep consistent.

**Guarantee-1 fix (the non-monotonicity Opus found in round 31)**: obligations are computed **per content-address**, as `present \ liveAddrs(H)`, where `liveAddrs(H)` includes `RETAINED_VERIFIED` references. A later disposal that crosses the VERIFIED boundary automatically materializes the obligation at that later `H` — it cannot "hide" behind a retained reference sharing the same content address, because the obligation is recomputed fresh at every hold rather than decided once and cached.

**Abort rule (corrected by the amendment — this supersedes the pre-amendment text)**: on abort at any point before step-6 durability, **re-run step 3** (the reclaim-obligation step), then release the lock `L`. Step 3 is idempotent: it removes both this hold's own temp file and, if the canonical rename already happened before the abort, the canonical-but-unreferenced candidate object — which is a member of `Obligations(H)` precisely because no durable event names it yet, justified by `INV-DURABLE-ONLY` (an object counts as live only if a durable event names it), not by any transaction-local knowledge of the abort. If the step-3 re-run itself fails with an I/O error, release the lock anyway — the obligation remains derivable from disk state, and the next hold's own step 3 is the backstop that will reclaim it. This is what makes Guarantee 1's postcondition (`present ⊆ liveAddrs(H)` after any hold in which step 3 completed) hold for **aborted** holds, not only successful ones — the specific gap the amendment closed.

**Crash-table addition**: for the "after rename, before step-6 durability" row — a crash in that window is reclaimed at the *next* hold (via its own step 3); a *clean* abort in that same window is reclaimed by the *same* hold's step-3 re-run (per the abort rule above) — these are two different recovery paths for adjacent but distinct failure timings, both converging on the same step-3 mechanism.

**Lock scope**: `L` is round 27/31's existing append lock, acquired through the same interface the appender itself uses — not a new lock or lock type. The reclamation sweeper and startup recovery are two more acquirers of this *same* lock. Startup recovery runs before any appender starts.

**Disposition of prior rounds' components**: every counterexample from rounds 28-31 is mapped, in Fable's reasoning, to the specific reason it can no longer occur under this design (each auxiliary record's failure mode is eliminated by retiring the record itself). Of the five items previously marked "carries forward unchanged" (see `.kstack/decisions/reasoning-effort-policy-2026-08-23-fable-round31-brief.md`'s summary): removal-last ordering and the equal-content guard are now subsumed by the recompute-at-every-hold model; the other three (immutable-refId/mutable-role split, the reprinted five refusal codes, and round 31's single never-split append-lock hold) are preserved, with the lock's hold **extended** (to include reclamation as its first step) rather than reopened or renegotiated.
